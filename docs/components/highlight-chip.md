# HighlightChip

A pill that floats over the map on glass and turns one highlight of the map on and off, such as line 5C in focus with everything outside it dimmed; while it is selected, the map shows the highlight.

<!-- run notes -->

The design is deferred (ADR 0010, `docs/adr/0010-unattended-runs-with-design-deferred.md`): there is no read-back, code map or export, and this spec decides what the drawing would, from the contract, the foundations, the tokens, the Base UI reference and the ROADMAP line that names it (`ROADMAP.md`, "Wave 3, interactive, on Base UI": "FilterChip and HighlightChip (Toggle)"). The extracted prototype component is not in any branch this run can reach. The foundations already place it: [Layout](../foundations/layout.md#stacking) stacks "the highlight chip" above the overview sheet and below the detail panel, with `z-index.highlight`; [Accessibility](../foundations/accessibility.md#rules) has Esc close "a dialog, then a detail panel, then a highlight"; and [Map](../foundations/map.md#focus-and-dimming) gives the dimming a line in focus brings.

## Anatomy

Penpot page `Components/HighlightChip`. Family: none (the contract's [Families](README.md#families) have no toggle; the chip takes the glass of a floating panel from [Elevation](../foundations/elevation.md#surfaces) and the pressed toggle's `color.surface.inverse` from the same page). Shared parts: none (every word is the caller's, and the selected state is told by `aria-pressed`, not by a hidden word).

HighlightChip is the one toggle that floats alone over the map and turns one map highlight on and off, such as a line in focus or the delayed vehicles; the screen places it (`z-index.highlight`, `size.panel-gap`) and clears it on Esc, while FilterChip is the chip in the top bar's filters that changes what the map and the lists show. Layout's stacking names one "highlight chip" between the sheet and the detail panel, Map's "Focus and dimming" is the highlight it turns on, a highlight keeps every vehicle on the map where a filter takes some away, and the registry gives both chips Base UI's `Toggle`, so they differ in what they change and where they sit, not in their behaviour.

The chip shows no close mark while selected: its selected state is told to the eye by the `color.surface.inverse` fill and to the ear by `aria-pressed`. The `close` icon is drawn only in the `CloseButton` shared part's folder (the contract's [Shared parts](README.md#shared-parts), held by the `shared-parts` check), a `CloseButton` can't sit inside the chip's `<button>`, and a chip whose press clears the highlight needs no second target; a close mark can join later as a shared part of its own.

The drawing has a `root` board holding one text layer, `label`, and no variant properties, so its variants are the values of its `State` property alone: `default`, `hover`, `focus`, `disabled`, `selected`, `selected-hover` and `selected-disabled`, named `Default`, `Hover`, `Focus`, `Disabled`, `Selected`, `SelectedHover` and `SelectedDisabled` (`penpot/README.md`, "Conventions"). `selected` is the chip turned on, Base UI's `[data-pressed]`; the press itself, `:active`, is drawn as hover.

## Base UI parts

Imported from `@base-ui/react/toggle`, at the version pinned in `packages/perron/package.json` (1.9.0 installed).

| Base UI part | Renders                                                                                                                                                                                                        | Perron sets                                                                                                                                                                                                               | Passed through                                                                                                 |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `Toggle`     | `<button type="button">`, with `aria-pressed`, `data-pressed` while selected, and `data-disabled` and the native `disabled` attribute while disabled; outside a `ToggleGroup`, as here, it keeps its own state | `className` (`pn-HighlightChip`, then the caller's), `style`, `ref`, `pressed` from `selected`, `defaultPressed` from `defaultSelected`, `onPressedChange` adapted from `onChange` (CR-3), `disabled`; `value` left unset | `nativeButton`, `render`, `onClick`, `aria-*` and every other prop of a native `<button>`, through `{...rest}` |

Base UI already does: toggling the pressed state on a click, Enter or Space, through the native button or, for a `render` that isn't a `<button>` with `nativeButton={false}`, through its own key handling; keeping the state itself when uncontrolled (`defaultPressed`) or following `pressed` when controlled; setting `aria-pressed` and `data-pressed` from that state; passing `onPressedChange` the next state and `details` whose `cancel()` stops the change; making a disabled toggle natively `disabled` (`focusableWhenDisabled` is fixed off in the installed `Toggle`), so it leaves the tab order and ignores clicks; and fixing `type="button"`, so the chip never submits a form it sits in.

## Props

In MUI's layout. Every prop the component declares gets this description as its JSDoc; the inherited rows keep Base UI's. No slots.

The chip takes `selected`, `defaultSelected` and `onChange(event, value, details)`, where `value` is the next selected state, a `boolean`, mapped onto Toggle's `pressed`, `defaultPressed` and `onPressedChange`, with none of Toggle's three names exposed. In the contract's [Design states](README.md#design-states), `pressed` names the momentary press (`:active`) and `selected` a chosen item; MUI's ToggleButton takes `selected`; FilterChip, built on the same `Toggle`, takes `selected` and `defaultSelected`; and the `props` check (`scripts/checks/props.ts`) takes `onChange` only with the parameters `(event, value, details)` or `(event)`. The contract's [Names from other libraries](README.md#names-from-other-libraries) leaves all three free, and Base UI's `onPressedChange` is adapted once, inside the chip, as its [State and events](README.md#state-and-events) asks.

Base UI's `value`, the string that names a toggle inside a `ToggleGroup`, is left out of the props type, and so is the native button's `value` with it: a HighlightChip stands alone, and SegmentedControl is the group of toggles. Base UI's function forms of `className` and `style` are left out too, so both are React's, as on every Perron component.

The chip comes in one look, with no `variant`, `color`, `size` or `startIcon`. The brand keeps colour for the map and its vehicles and the yellow for the one main action, a screen has one highlight chip, a badge or an icon beside the words goes in `children` (such as a LineBadge before "Line 5C"), and each prop can join later without a break.

`children` is required: a chip with no words has no name, and the selected fill alone would say nothing. The chip takes no form `name` of its own and renders no hidden input: its state changes the map, not what a form sends, and the native button's `name`, which reaches the `<button>` with the other native props, is never sent, since a `type="button"` never submits.

## Tokens

One row per part, state and property. States are the contract's design states, and Penpot's `State` values map to them: `default` is rest, no selector; `hover` is `:hover` inside `@media (hover: hover)`, and the press, `:active`, takes the same values; `focus` is `:focus-visible`; `disabled` is `[data-disabled]`; `selected` is Base UI's `[data-pressed]`, which the installed `Toggle` sets for as long as the chip is on, so here it is the selected state and the press itself is `:active` alone. Every `color` row sets the root's `color`, which the label and any icon in it take.

The chip is glass at rest, as a floating panel is (`color.surface.overlay` with `effect.glass-blur` and `effect.glass-saturate`, `shadow.panel` and a `border.width.025` edge in `color.border.subtle`), and `color.surface.inverse` with `color.text.inverse` while selected, keeping its shadow and turning its edge `transparent`. The chip floats alone over the map, where [Elevation](../foundations/elevation.md#surfaces) puts everything on glass and lets only floating panels cast `shadow.panel`, and the same page gives "pressed toggles" `color.surface.inverse`, so a selected chip reads as on over land, water and parks alike.

The chip is `size.control` tall at least and as wide at least, with `space.300` at the sides, `space.150` between the nodes of its `children`, `typography.label` and `icon.size.400` for an icon in it. `size.control` is the height of "icon buttons and filter chips" and of the map controls' buttons beside which the chip floats, `space.300` is a small button's and a segment's inline padding, `typography.label`'s description names chips, and `icon.size.400` is the step for icons "inside controls".

A selected chip that is disabled takes `color.surface.inset` with `color.text.tertiary`, as Button's disabled solid button does, while a disabled chip at rest keeps its glass with `color.text.tertiary`. The contract makes disabled a colour, never an opacity; the inset fill still sets a selected chip apart from the glass of one at rest, so a highlight held on, such as while the map follows a vehicle, still shows; and `color.text.tertiary` on `color.surface.inverse` would read as a live chip with faint words.

## Keyboard and ARIA

From the Base UI reference for `Toggle` and the installed `Toggle`, which builds on Base UI's button hook. "From" says who does it.

| Key              | Does                                                                                                                           | From                                                   |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| `Tab`            | Moves focus to the chip, in reading order; a disabled chip is skipped                                                          | The browser                                            |
| `Shift+Tab`      | Moves focus back out of the chip                                                                                               | The browser                                            |
| `Enter`, `Space` | Toggle the chip and call `onChange` with the next state                                                                        | The browser's click; Base UI for a non-button `render` |
| `Esc`            | Nothing on the chip: the screen clears the highlight on Esc, after a dialog and a detail panel, and sets `selected` to `false` | The screen                                             |

| Element | Role                                      | Accessible name from                 | States announced                                                |
| ------- | ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `root`  | `button`, a toggle through `aria-pressed` | Its content: the caller's `children` | `aria-pressed` (pressed or not pressed), disabled when disabled |

The chip tells its state with `aria-pressed` and the selected fill, with no word of its own, leaving the caller free to change `children` with the state, as "Follow" and "Following" do. [Accessibility](../foundations/accessibility.md#rules) wants a toggle's state "in words as well as in looks", and `aria-pressed` puts it in words for the ear; what the chip highlights is the caller's to say, an owned "On" would read beside "Line 5C" as a second name, and a selected-state label can join later as a new optional prop.

What axe won't catch, from the contract, each with how this component meets it:

- [ ] Everything the eye is told, the ear is told, for every shape each prop accepts: the caller's `children` name what the chip highlights, and the selected state the eye gets from the inverse fill the ear gets from `aria-pressed`; a LineBadge or icon in `children` brings its own text or is `aria-hidden` beside words that say it.
- [ ] Every landmark and named region has an accessible name: none here; the chip is a button, not a region.
- [ ] Every default label can be overridden: none here; the chip owns no strings.
- [ ] Every shape the type accepts renders, in every variant: `children` as a string, an element, or a list of an element and a string, each inside the one root, in every `State`.
- [ ] A hidden copy kept for animation is `aria-hidden`, and a collapsed region is `inert`: none here; the chip doesn't animate.
- [ ] Native semantics first: a native `<button type="button">` from Base UI's `Toggle`, with `aria-pressed`, the one ARIA attribute, set by Base UI.
- [ ] Focus moves in reading order and returns on close: the chip is one tab stop where the screen puts it in the document; it opens nothing, so nothing returns focus, and toggling it keeps focus on it.
- [ ] Every target is at least `size.target-min` square: the chip is at least `size.control` tall and wide, above `size.target-min`.
- [ ] Loops stop when the system asks for less motion: none here; the chip has no motion.

## Component rules

- **CR-1.** HighlightChip renders Base UI's `Toggle` as its root, with `{...rest}` first and its own `className`, `style`, `ref` and state props after, as the contract's [Root](README.md#root) asks, so `className` reads `pn-HighlightChip` and then the caller's. `selected`, `defaultSelected`, `onChange` and `disabled` are taken out of `rest` before the spread and given to `Toggle` as `pressed`, `defaultPressed`, `onPressedChange` (CR-3) and `disabled`, and `className`, `style` and `ref` are taken out and given to it as they are, `className` merged.
- **CR-2.** The chip keeps no state of its own: Base UI's `Toggle` holds it, uncontrolled from `defaultSelected` or controlled by `selected`, and sets `aria-pressed` and `data-pressed` from it. The CSS reads the selected state from `[data-pressed]` alone, never from a class or a prop.
- **CR-3.** `onChange(event, value, details)` is Base UI's `onPressedChange(pressed, details)` adapted once, inside the chip: `event` is `details.event`, `value` is the next selected state, and `details` is passed through unchanged, so `details.cancel()` keeps the chip as it was. The props type declares none of `pressed`, `defaultPressed`, `onPressedChange` and `value`.
- **CR-4.** Every state keeps one geometry: the root draws its `border.width.025` edge in every state, `color.border.subtle` at rest and `transparent` while selected, and the same padding and minimum size, so turning the chip on never moves it or what floats beside it.
- **CR-5.** In Windows' forced colours, where fills are dropped, a selected chip that is not disabled sets `background-color: Highlight`, `color: HighlightText` and `forced-color-adjust: none` inside `@media (forced-colors: active)`, on hover and press too, so the system keeps the fill and the selected and unselected chip still look apart; a selected disabled chip sets a `GrayText` fill with `Canvas` words under `forced-color-adjust: none`, as FilterChip's and SegmentedControl's do, so the selected state still shows when the chip is disabled; and, since `forced-color-adjust: none` keeps the focus ring in its token's colour too, a selected chip's `:focus-visible` ring sets `outline-color: CanvasText`, so it still shows ([Accessibility](../foundations/accessibility.md#what-a-person-checks)).
- **CR-6.** Hover is `:hover` inside `@media (hover: hover)`, and it and `:active` lay no layer on a disabled chip: the hover rule reads `.pn-HighlightChip:not([data-disabled])`.
- **CR-7.** The chip never cuts its words off: it sets no `white-space: nowrap` and no `text-overflow`. Alone in a container narrower than it, at 320 px or at 200% zoom, it wraps inside itself and grows taller, a word longer than the line breaking within it with `overflow-wrap: anywhere`, since `break-word` leaves the anonymous flex item that holds the words as wide as its longest word, and stays inside the container through `max-inline-size: 100%` ([Accessibility](../foundations/accessibility.md#what-a-person-checks), zoom and reflow).
- **CR-8.** The chip sets no `position`, `z-index` or margin: the screen that floats it over the map places it, with `z-index.highlight`, which the contract's [Token families](README.md#token-families) keep for patterns, and `size.panel-gap`, the distance a floating panel keeps from the viewport's edge and from the other panels, which the chip can't measure from inside itself, since where it sits is the screen's. The chip's CSS reads only the component row's tokens.
- **CR-9.** The chip doesn't listen for Esc and doesn't clear itself: clearing the highlight on Esc, after a dialog and a detail panel, is the screen's ([Accessibility](../foundations/accessibility.md#rules)), which sets `selected` to `false`.
- **CR-10.** The chip's own props are only those [Props](#props) gives: `children` is required in the type, Toggle's `pressed`, `defaultPressed`, `onPressedChange` and `value` and the function forms of `className` and `style` are left out, and there is no `variant`, `color`, `size`, `startIcon` or `slotProps`.
- **CR-11.** The chip renders only the caller's `children` inside its root: no close mark while selected, no `close` icon, which the `CloseButton` shared part alone draws, and no owned or visually hidden word for its state, which the selected fill tells the eye and `aria-pressed` the ear.

## Do and don't

For a caller using the component, with the reason.

| Do                                                                                                                                 | Don't                                                                                                                                                    |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Name what the chip highlights in a word or two: "Line 5C", "Delayed vehicles".                                                     | Write an instruction such as "Click to highlight": `aria-pressed` already says it is a toggle and whether it is on.                                      |
| Float one chip over the map, placed by the screen with `z-index.highlight`, and clear it on Esc after a dialog and a detail panel. | Put a row of highlight chips in a panel or the top bar: a filter people turn on and off there is a FilterChip, and a choice of views a SegmentedControl. |
| Control it with `selected` when the map can change the highlight too, such as a click on another line.                             | Keep a second copy of the state beside an uncontrolled chip: the two drift apart, and the chip says the highlight is on when it is not.                  |
| Put a LineBadge or an icon in `children` beside the words when it helps the eye find the line.                                     | Give the chip a badge or an icon alone: its content is its name, and a screen reader needs the words.                                                    |
| Change the words with the state when the action reads better that way, as "Follow" and "Following" do.                             | Restyle the selected chip through `className` to look like the unselected one: the inverse fill is what tells the eye the highlight is on.               |
| Let the chip wrap in a narrow container: it grows taller and keeps every word.                                                     | Cut its words off with `nowrap` and an ellipsis through `className`: a Danish line name at 320 px would lose its end.                                    |
