# DepartureRow

One departure from a stop, as one row of the stop's list of departures: the line's badge and where it goes, how fresh the vehicle's data is, and how soon it leaves, with its timetabled time and how late it runs, or, for a line that runs to an interval, how often it runs. A cancelled departure stays in the list, marked "Cancelled", and a train's platform or track can join the row's second line.

The design is deferred under ADR 0010 (`docs/adr/0010-unattended-runs-with-design-deferred.md`), so there is no read-back, code map or export: this spec decides the variant property, the layers, the design states and the token for every part. Its sources are the [contract](../components/README.md) and its Patterns section, the [foundations](../foundations/README.md), [Data states](../foundations/data-states.md), [Typography](../foundations/typography.md#roles), [content design](../content-design.md), [Accessibility](../foundations/accessibility.md), `list_tokens`, and the specs of the three components in `dependsOn`, [LineBadge](../components/line-badge.md), [Delay](../components/delay.md) and [VehicleDot](../components/vehicle-dot.md). The ROADMAP names it first in 7.2, Patterns, and no more. The extracted prototype is not reachable from this branch, so nothing here rests on it. It follows the merged patterns' precedents: [DetailHeader](detail-header.md)'s `line` and `lineName`, [StopTimeline](stop-timeline.md)'s `time` and `delay` and its Delay label props, and [DisruptionCard](disruption-card.md)'s `slotProps` keyed by the pattern's own parts. VehicleDotLabel and VehicleDotLegend, the siblings in flight, are on their branches with their design deferred and their briefs done. The row's dot and word keep VehicleDotLabel's names, `variant`, `line` and the five state labels, its dot at VehicleDot's `size='small'` and its `aria-hidden`; where VehicleDotLabel, on its branch, sets the word in `typography.label-sm` and `color.text.primary`, the row's word is its second line, in the Rows family's `typography.caption` and `color.text.secondary`.

## Anatomy

Penpot page `Patterns/DepartureRow`. Family: none, since the row is not pressed and draws no fill, border or radius of its own; its text follows the Rows family's text, a name in `typography.label` and its second line in `typography.caption` with `color.text.secondary`. Shared parts: `VisuallyHidden`, through the `UnknownValue` helper, for a countdown no one knows.

The row is drawn as a grid of three columns and two lines: the line's `badge` at the start, centred on both lines; the `destination` over the `status`, the vehicle's dot and its state's word and then the `platform`, in the middle; and the `countdown` over the `schedule`, the timetabled `time` and the `delay`, at the end, or the `interval` in their place. It has no tag, no action and no "Show on map" of its own. The line, the destination and how soon it leaves are what a rider scans a stop's departures for, so they sit on the first line, the start and the end; [Data states](../foundations/data-states.md) asks that a vehicle's state be written in words next to its dot, and [Typography](../foundations/typography.md#roles) gives the countdown `typography.data-lg` and "the time under a countdown" `typography.data-sm`, so the second line holds the state's word under the destination and the time under the countdown; a tag such as "Night bus" and an action are what no source asks of the row yet, and each can join later as an optional prop without a break.

Three additions came later, each an optional prop, for Mylder's stop departures, which list cancelled trips, the Metro's interval and a train's platform. A **cancelled** departure keeps its row, so a missing bus reads as missing rather than gone: "Cancelled" takes the dot's and the state's word's place, since no vehicle is coming to be fresh about, and the countdown and the time are struck through, what the rider would have caught (CR-13). A line that runs to an **interval**, as the Copenhagen Metro does, has no clock times to count down to, so "Every 3 min", or "Every 3 to 4 min", takes the countdown's place in its type, and the second line at the end holds nothing (CR-14). A **platform** or track, which Rejseplanen's departure boards give for trains, sits after the state's word on the second line, in the middle column, where it can wrap at 320 px while the countdown's column never does (CR-15).

The drawing is a `root` board holding ten layers in reading order: `badge`, `destination`, `status` (holding `dot`, the state's word and `platform`), `countdown` or `interval`, and `schedule` (holding `time` and `delay`).

The row takes the vehicle's data state as `variant`, typed as VehicleDot's `variant` with its five values, `'live'`, `'ageing'`, `'estimated'`, `'timetable-only'` and `'no-signal'`, default `'live'`, so the Penpot `variant` property has those five values and the drawing five variants, `Live`, `Ageing`, `Estimated`, `TimetableOnly` and `NoSignal`. The state changes the row's drawing (the dot's shape, the word, the "~" and the unknown delay), so it is the Penpot variant property, which the contract names `variant`; VehicleDot takes the same values under the same name, and the contract gives `state` to CSS states; the default is the first value, as the contract and VehicleDot set it. `cancelled` is a boolean, default `false`, that changes the drawing too, the word, its colour and the strike-through, so it is a Penpot boolean property of the same name and sets the bare `data-cancelled` on the root, as the contract's Styling asks of a boolean that changes the look; `interval` and `platform` are content, not properties.

## Props

In MUI's layout. Every prop DepartureRow declares gets this description as its JSDoc. Its props type is the `<div>`'s props, `ComponentPropsWithRef<'div'>`, without `children`, with its own members added; the `<div>`'s other attributes reach the root and get no row, as in MUI's tables. No `*Label` prop for a word the caller gives, and no `render` (see [Base UI parts](#base-ui-parts)).

The row takes the departure as flat props: `line` and `lineName` as DetailHeader takes them, `destination` (a `ReactNode`, required), `countdown` (the seconds until it leaves, as expected, a `number`), `time` (the timetabled time as `HH:MM`, optional) and `delay` (minutes, `number | null`, optional, as StopTimeline's stops and Delay's `minutes` take it), rather than one `departure` object or a `Date`. DetailHeader set that the patterns after it take a line as `line` and `lineName`; flat props each get a row, a JSDoc and a default; `time` and `delay` keep StopTimeline's shapes, so a stop's departures and a trip's stops are fed alike; and the row takes the countdown as a number the screen works out from its own clock, so the pattern holds no clock or timer and renders the same on the server and the client.

The three later additions are flat props too. `cancelled` is a boolean, an adjective with no `is` in front, as the contract names booleans. `interval` takes the minutes between departures, as a number or a `[fewest, most]` pair, in minutes as `delay` takes them, since a headway is a fact about the line rather than a moment the screen's clock counts down to. `platform` is a `ReactNode` the caller writes whole, "Track 4", "Platform 2" or "Spor 4", not a number the row puts a word before: the contract's Words gives visible content to the caller, and whether a stop calls it a track, a platform or a stand, and in which language, is the operator's data, which Rejseplanen gives as the board shows it, not one word the row could own. `countdown` was required and is now optional, a change the contract lets the API make, since an interval row has no countdown to give; left out, it shows "—" as a number that isn't finite does.

The row takes every label its words need as its own props: the five state labels (`liveLabel` to `noSignalLabel`, given to the state's word and to the dot), `nowLabel`, `unknownLabel`, `cancelledLabel`, `everyLabel`, `everyRangeLabel`, and Delay's five (`minutesUnitLabel`, also the countdown's unit, `minutesLateLabel`, `minutesEarlyLabel`, `onTimeLabel` and `delayUnknownLabel`, given to the Delay), with `slotProps.dot` and `slotProps.delay` leaving those labels out. The contract asks that every default label the row shows can be overridden on one instance; StopTimeline takes Delay's five under Delay's names, so a caller learns them once; the state's word is the row's own text, so its labels are the row's, and one `minutesUnitLabel` keeps the countdown's "min" and the Delay's the same word.

No `size`: the row is one size, set for a list in a panel. No `color`: the line's colours are LineBadge's and VehicleDot's and the delay's is Delay's.

## Tokens

One row per part, state and property. DepartureRow is not interactive, so its one design state is rest (CR-8); the `variant` values change only the composed components and the words, and `cancelled`, a state of the data rather than of the pointer, adds the rows marked "cancelled". LineBadge, VehicleDot and Delay keep their own tokens, as their specs give them; rows marked "through" are theirs, listed so the drawing has them.

The row spaces its parts with `space.300` between its three columns, `space.050` between its two lines, `space.200` above and below its content, `space.100` between the dot and its word and between the time and the delay, and has no divider, fill or radius of its own. `space.300` is what Semantic spacing gives between parts of a detail panel, where a stop's departures sit, and what StopTimeline sets between its columns; `space.200` above and below gives two rows 16 px apart, the 16 px StopTimeline's stops get from their `space.150`, rounded to the scale with the badge's 22 px; `space.100` is the gap of a dot beside its word and of a figure beside another; a divider between rows is the list's, which holds the rows in its own `<li>`s, as the Rows family says "where the design divides rows".

The `time` is set in `typography.data-sm`, and the Delay is brought to `typography.data-sm` beside it through a rule on `pn-DepartureRow-delay` inside `pn-DepartureRow-schedule` that outranks `.pn-Delay`'s font (CR-12). [Typography](../foundations/typography.md#roles) gives `typography.data-sm` to "the time under a countdown", and Delay's spec lets a caller set its font through `className`; a delay at `typography.data`, 12 px, beside an 11 px time would read as two sizes on one line.

The `interval` takes every row of the `countdown`'s, in one rule with it, so an interval and the countdowns above and below it in a list share a column, a type and an end (CR-14). The `platform` keeps the status's `typography.caption` but takes `color.text.primary`, since it is a fact the rider acts on beside a note on how sure the row is, and sits `space.100` further from the state's word than the gap the dot keeps, `space.200` in all, so "Live" and "Track 4" read as two things (CR-15). A cancelled row sets the status's word in `color.text.danger`, the colour Semantic colours gives to late and to delay disruptions, which reaches 4.5:1 on every surface, and strikes through the countdown, the interval and the time with `text-decoration-line: line-through`, in their own colours (CR-13); the platform keeps `color.text.primary`, since where the bus would have left from is still so.

- [ ] Everything the eye is told, the ear is told, for every shape each prop accepts: the line's colour is named by `lineName`, read from the badge; the dot's shape by the state's word beside it, read once, since the dot is `aria-hidden` (CR-6); the "~" by that word, "Estimated", "Timetable only" or "No signal" (CR-4); the countdown by its figure and "min", or "Now"; an unknown countdown by "Unknown" (CR-3); the delay by Delay's own words, and a timetable-only or no-signal vehicle's "—" by "Delay unknown" (CR-5); a cancelled row's strike-through and danger colour by "Cancelled" (CR-13); an interval by its words, read whole (CR-14).
- [ ] Every landmark and named region has an accessible name: none here; the row is no region, and a list of rows is the caller's.
- [ ] Every default label can be overridden: `nowLabel`, `unknownLabel`, `cancelledLabel`, `everyLabel`, `everyRangeLabel`, the five state labels from `liveLabel` to `noSignalLabel`, and Delay's five, `minutesUnitLabel`, `minutesLateLabel`, `minutesEarlyLabel`, `onTimeLabel` and `delayUnknownLabel`.
- [ ] Every shape the type accepts renders, in every variant: `lineName` and `destination` render a string, an element and a list alike; `countdown` positive, under 45 seconds, zero, negative, fractional (rounded) and not finite (CR-3); `time` given and left out; `delay` a number, `null` and left out, in every `variant`; a long destination wraps under itself (CR-9); `interval` one number, a range, a range given high to low, a range whose ends round alike, and not finite (CR-14); `cancelled` with a countdown and with an interval (CR-13); `platform` a string and an element, beside a long state's word at 320 px (CR-15).
- [ ] A hidden copy kept for animation is `aria-hidden`, and a collapsed region is `inert`: none here; nothing animates or collapses. The `dot` and the "~" are `aria-hidden` because a word says them, not for animation.
- [ ] Native semantics first: plain `div`s and `span`s, a `<time>` for the time, and the composed components' own; no ARIA but `aria-hidden` on the dot, the "~" and the unknown "—". A cancelled countdown and time are struck through by CSS, not wrapped in `<s>` or `<del>`, which screen readers announce unevenly or not at all; the word "Cancelled" carries it.
- [ ] Focus moves in reading order and returns on close: the row takes no focus; its DOM order, badge, destination, state, platform, countdown or interval, time and delay, is its reading order on screen.
- [ ] Every target is at least `size.target-min` square: no target; the row is not pressed (CR-8).
- [ ] Loops stop when the system asks for less motion: none here; nothing moves. A countdown that changes is new props from the screen, not an animation.

## Component rules

Rules for this pattern alone, numbered so the build and the review can cite them.

- **CR-1.** The pattern imports only `LineBadge`, `VehicleDot` and `Delay`, the `UnknownValue` helper, `classes`, `given` and `interpolate` from `packages/perron/src/internal/`, `useOwnedStrings` from `PerronProvider`, React and its own CSS, as the contract's [Patterns](../components/README.md#patterns) section says; never `@base-ui/react`. The types of `line`, `variant` and the `slotProps` come through type-only imports of `LineBadgeProps`, `VehicleDotProps` and `DelayProps`.
- **CR-2.** The root is a `<div>` with `className` `pn-DepartureRow` then the caller's, and always carries `data-variant`, the default `live` included, and `data-cancelled` only when `cancelled` (CR-13). The caller's rest props are spread on the root and the row's own class and data attribute after them, so the row's win; every prop the props type declares, `variant`, `line`, `lineName`, `destination`, `countdown`, `time`, `delay`, `interval`, `cancelled`, `platform`, every `*Label` prop and `slotProps`, is taken out of the rest, so none reaches the DOM.
- **CR-3.** The countdown comes from `countdown` alone. Left out, or a number that isn't finite (`NaN`, `Infinity`), it shows `UnknownValue` with the `unknown` word: "—" for the eye, "Unknown" for the ear. Under 45 seconds, zero and negative included, it shows the `now` word. Otherwise it shows the whole minutes and, after a text space, the `minutesUnit` word, the number and the word in separate text runs: "4 min". It never shows seconds or a clock time in place of the minutes.

  The whole minutes are `Math.round(countdown / 60)`, so 89 seconds show "1 min" and 90 seconds "2 min", with no upper bound past which the row shows a clock time instead. Delay rounds its minutes with `Math.round`, so the countdown and the delay round alike; from 45 seconds, where "Now" ends, rounding gives "1 min" with no gap; and a departure an hour off still reads as minutes, with its timetabled time under it, so the row needs no second format.

- **CR-4.** A "~" comes straight before the countdown's figure when `variant` is `estimated`, `timetable-only` or `no-signal`, in its own `aria-hidden` span, and never before "Now", an unknown countdown, or a `live` or `ageing` one. Content design asks for "~" before estimated times; a vehicle placed from the timetable and the latest delay, one with no real-time data and one silent past the app's ageing threshold ([Thresholds](../foundations/data-states.md#thresholds)) all leave at a time worked out rather than reported, while a live or ageing report places the vehicle itself; the state's word beside the dot says the same to the ear, so the "~" needs no word of its own; and "Now" and "—" are no time to qualify.
- **CR-5.** A `timetable-only` or `no-signal` row always renders its Delay with `minutes={null}`, whatever `delay` holds and when `delay` is left out, as [Data states](../foundations/data-states.md#delays) says those states always show an unknown delay. Any other row renders its Delay with `minutes={delay}` only when `delay` is given, `null` included; left out, it renders no Delay. A cancelled row (CR-13) and an interval row (CR-14) render no Delay, in any `variant`, whatever `delay` holds.
- **CR-6.** Unless `cancelled` (CR-13), the `status` part shows the `dot`, a VehicleDot with the row's `variant` and `line`, `size='small'`, `aria-hidden="true"` and the five state labels, then the state's owned word, with its `*Label` override laid over it. The word is visible text, so a screen reader hears the state once, from the word, as VehicleDot's CR-6 asks of a caller that writes the word beside the dot.
- **CR-7.** Each inner part's props are its `slotProps` value with the row's own laid over it: its `className` joins after the part's class (`pn-DepartureRow-<part>`, then the caller's), and its other own props (`line`, `size`, `variant`, `aria-hidden`, `minutes`, `dateTime`, the labels and the children) win. The pattern joins them by hand, with `classes`, since it can't import `mergeProps`.
- **CR-8.** The row is text: no part is a link or a button, it has no hover, pressed, focus or selected look, no tab stop and no role. A screen that opens a departure puts a Perron link or button beside the list, or makes its own list item the link, and the row has no pointer feedback to draw. DepartureRow is not a live region either: a screen that updates its departures announces a change of state, such as a departure leaving or turning to no signal, in its own polite live region, never every minute's countdown ([Accessibility](../foundations/accessibility.md#what-a-person-checks)).
- **CR-9.** The row sizes to its container, with no width of its own. The middle column is `minmax(0, 1fr)`, so the destination and the state's word wrap long words (`overflow-wrap: anywhere`) within it at 320 px, while the badge, the countdown and the schedule never wrap or shrink.
- **CR-10.** The `time` part renders as `<time dateTime={time}>{time}</time>`, the `HH:MM` given. The `schedule` part renders only when it holds a `time` or a Delay (CR-5); with neither, the countdown stands alone at the end and the second line holds the state's word only. With an `interval`, no `time` renders, so no `schedule` either (CR-14).
- **CR-11.** The stories use only lines whose badge pairs reach 4.5:1 (M2, M3, E, F, H, bus and regional), so axe's `color-contrast` rule stays on in every story; M1, M4, A, B and C miss it by rule, as LineBadge's CR-6 says.
- **CR-12.** The `delay` part's font and tracking are `typography.data-sm`, the time's, set in a rule whose selector outranks `.pn-Delay`'s own, `.pn-DepartureRow-schedule .pn-DepartureRow-delay`, the delay read inside its `schedule`, so the order the two stylesheets load in can't undo it; the contract's selectors are one class, then attributes, so the rule reaches that rank through the `schedule` part it sits in, as `.pn-Alert[data-color] .pn-Alert-icon` reaches its part, never through two classes on one element; Delay keeps its colour, and the rule sets its `tabular-nums` and its `nowrap` again, since the `font` shorthand resets the first and the contract asks a rule that sets a role to decide long words.
- **CR-13.** `cancelled` keeps the row and every part it would have, but for three changes: the `status` shows the `cancelled` word, with `cancelledLabel` laid over it, in place of the `dot` and the state's word, so no dot renders and the state's word is never read; no Delay renders (CR-5); and the root carries the bare `data-cancelled`, under which the `status` is set in `color.text.danger` and the `countdown`, the `interval` and the `time` are struck through, their text and its "~" unchanged, so the rider still sees what the row would have left at. The strike-through is CSS alone, never `<s>` or `<del>`; the word, read before the countdown, says it to the ear. `data-variant` stays, since the caller's `variant` is still the data's. With `cancelled` left out or `false`, nothing changes and no `data-cancelled` is set.
- **CR-14.** `interval` replaces the `countdown` part with the `interval` part and leaves out the `time`, the `delay` and so the `schedule`, whatever `countdown`, `time` and `delay` hold and in every `variant`; the dot and the state's word stay, since a Metro train's data is fresh or not as a bus's is. One number is a range whose ends are the same. The two ends are rounded to whole minutes with `Math.round`, as the countdown is, and shown fewest first, whichever order the pair comes in: the `every` template with `{count}` the minutes when the two ends are the same, and the `everyRange` template with `{from}` the fewest and `{to}` the most when they differ, each filled by `interpolate`: "Every 3 min", "Every 3 to 4 min". An end that isn't finite shows `UnknownValue` with the `unknown` word, as CR-3 does. No "~" comes before an interval, even for an estimate: it is how often the line runs, not when one departure leaves.
- **CR-15.** `platform` renders in its own `span`, the `platform` part, as the last child of the `status`, after the state's word or the `cancelled` word and a text space, only when it is given (`given`); its text is the caller's, whole. The text space keeps the word and the platform two words to a screen reader and to `textContent`, and the flex row the `status` is drops it from the drawing, so the gap is the tokens' alone. It takes `color.text.primary` and `space.200` from the word before it, the status's `space.100` gap and its own `space.100` margin, and wraps with the status (`overflow-wrap: anywhere`) inside the middle column at 320 px.

## Stories

Stories are titled `Patterns/DepartureRow`. No Penpot design yet, so the variant stories are named by the variant names the design will have, the `variant` values in PascalCase (the Conventions in `penpot/README.md`). ## Do and don't

For a caller using the pattern, with the reason.

| Do                                                                                                                                                                                                             | Don't                                                                                                                      |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Work out `variant` from the departing vehicle's last report, with the app's [thresholds](../foundations/data-states.md#thresholds), by default `vehicle-dot.live-threshold` and `vehicle-dot.ageing-threshold` | Pass `live` for a departure no vehicle reports on yet: the row would claim more than the data knows; pass `timetable-only` |
| Give `countdown` as the seconds to the expected departure, worked out from the screen's clock, and update it as the clock moves                                                                                | Pass the timetabled seconds for a late departure: the countdown and the delay would disagree                               |
| Give the timetabled `time` and let the Delay say how late it runs                                                                                                                                              | Pass the expected time as `time` as well as a delay: the rider adds the delay twice                                        |
| Set a stop's departures as a `<ul>`, each row in its own `<li>`, under a Section that names the stop or the platform                                                                                           | Put a list of rows in a live region: every countdown's change would be read                                                |
| Keep `lineName` the short name riders know and `destination` the place: "5C" towards "Husum Torv"                                                                                                              | Put the line in `destination`, "5C Husum Torv": the badge already reads "5C" first                                         |
| Open a departure from a Perron link or button the screen sets beside the list, or from the list item made a link                                                                                               | Add `onClick` or `tabIndex` to the row: it has no focus ring, role or pressed look                                         |
| Keep a cancelled departure in the list with `cancelled`, where it would have been                                                                                                                              | Drop it from the list: a rider waiting for it reads its absence as a bus not yet shown                                     |
| Give `interval` for a line that runs to a headway, such as the Metro, and leave out `time`                                                                                                                     | Pass a countdown worked out from a headway: it claims a departure no timetable sets                                        |
| Write `platform` whole, as the board gives it: "Track 4", "Spor 4"                                                                                                                                             | Pass the number alone, "4": the rider can't tell a track from a minute                                                     |
