# DetailPanel

The panel that opens on the right of the map when a rider picks a vehicle or a stop: a Sheet headed by a DetailHeader, which names the line's vehicle or the stop, with the close button at the end of its header row and the caller's details below, and the bottom sheet it becomes on a phone.

The design is deferred (ADR 0010, `docs/adr/0010-unattended-runs-with-design-deferred.md`): this spec decides what the drawing would, from the [contract](../components/README.md) and its Patterns section, the [foundations](../foundations/README.md), [content design](../content-design.md), the tokens and the specs of the two entries in `dependsOn`, [Sheet](../components/sheet.md) and [DetailHeader](detail-header.md). The extracted prototype is not reachable from this branch, so nothing here rests on it. What the foundations already say of the detail panel is that it sits on the right ([Layout](../foundations/layout.md#page-shell)), is `size.detail-width` wide on a wide screen, stacks at `z-index.detail` ([Layout](../foundations/layout.md#stacking)), becomes a bottom sheet under `breakpoint.phone`, sets its parts `space.300` apart ([Semantic spacing](../foundations/semantic-spacing.md#space)), and closes on Esc after a dialog and before a highlight ([Accessibility](../foundations/accessibility.md#rules)).

## Anatomy

Penpot page `Patterns/DetailPanel`. Family: Panels, through [Sheet](../components/sheet.md), whose popup draws the Panels row's glass, border, `radius.xl` and `shadow.panel`; the pattern draws no surface of its own. Shared parts: `CloseButton`, through the Sheet's close button; the `VisuallyHidden` span DetailHeader composes stays inside it.

The drawing will have a `root` board, an instance of Sheet at `anchor` `right`, holding three layers: `header`, an instance of DetailHeader; `close`, the Sheet's close button after it in the same row; and `content`, the caller's body below.

- The DetailHeader goes in the Sheet's `title` slot, rendered as a `div` through the Sheet's `slotProps.title.render`, with the panel's `aria-labelledby` pointing at the DetailHeader's `h2`, and the Sheet's own close button stays at the end of the header row after it, not in DetailHeader's `action`. The Sheet always draws its header with `title` and its close button, and a pattern can't import Base UI to render `Drawer.Close` into DetailHeader's `action`, which DetailHeader's spec assumed; the `title` slot keeps the header in place while the body scrolls, where a DetailHeader at the top of `children` would scroll away and repeat the Sheet's heading. `Drawer.Title` is an `h2`, which can't hold DetailHeader's `div`s and `h2`, so it renders as a `div`, and Base UI's `aria-labelledby` would then name the dialog from the whole header, subheader and action included, so the panel passes its own, which Base UI merges last. DetailHeader's `action` keeps the caller's other controls. The build then rewrites the Sheet's `title` description in Sheet's code and in its spec, "passes its name as `title` and the rest of its header in `children`", to say this.
- The Sheet's `backdrop` is not one of the panel's parts: the panel is never modal (CR-4), so the Sheet renders none. The Sheet's `header` row and `title` part are the pattern's own, set by CR-2, and get no `slotProps` key.

Base UI already does, through the Sheet: the `dialog` role, named here by the panel's own `aria-labelledby`, which Base UI's Drawer merges after its own (`DrawerPopup` takes the element props last), so it wins over the title `div`'s `id`; moving focus to the popup on open and back to the trigger or the element focused before on close; closing the topmost open sheet on Esc, so an open dialog closes before the panel; swipe to dismiss towards its edge; a bottom sheet's snap points; and the open and close slide. The Sheet adds, on top: the body's Tab stop while it scrolls, the non-modal panel that leaves the map usable, and the close button's owned label.

- DetailPanel has no `render`, on the root or in `slotProps`, as DetailHeader and KioskBar have none: the contract's `render` is Base UI's, inherited from a Base UI props type, which a pattern can't import, so `slotProps.header` leaves out the `render` DetailHeader doesn't have either, and the Sheet's parts' props are given on as they are.

`DetailPanelProps` extends the `div`'s props, `ComponentPropsWithRef<'div'>`, less `title` and `children`, which it declares as its own, as DetailHeader's does: `className`, `style`, `ref` and the other `div` props reach the root, the Sheet's popup, as the contract's [Root](../components/README.md#root) says. Every member above is taken out of the rest props first, so the HTML `title` attribute never reaches the element. The types of the Sheet's props come from `SheetProps` and `DetailHeaderProps` by type-only imports, which the contract's Patterns section allows. No `variant` beyond DetailHeader's, no `color` or `size`: the panel has one look per anchor.

- The panel leaves out the Sheet's `modal`, so it is never modal: the detail panel sits beside the map while the rider pans and picks other vehicles, the Sheet's own Do and don't keeps a modal sheet for what is a Dialog, and the Sheet's default, false, is what the panel always passes.
- The panel leaves out the Sheet's `initialFocus`, `finalFocus` and `render`, which the Sheet inherits from `Drawer.Popup`, by extending the `div`'s props rather than `SheetProps`: Base UI's defaults are what the panel needs, focus to the popup on open and back to the trigger or the element focused before, such as the vehicle list's row, on close; a pattern takes no `render`; and a member can join later without a break, where taking one out is one.
- The panel takes a `slotProps` key for each of its parts but the root, `header`, `trigger`, `portal`, `viewport`, `close` and `content`, each given on to DetailHeader or to the Sheet's key of the same name, and none for the Sheet's `title` and `backdrop`, as KioskBar and DetailHeader give one per part, `slotProps.portal.container` is how a screen puts the panel in its own shell, and the Sheet's `title` is the panel's own (CR-2), and its `backdrop` never renders (CR-4).

One row per part, state and property the pattern sets itself. The panel has no interactive state of its own, so every row is rest.

- The content is a grid of one column, `minmax(0, 1fr)`, its rows packed at the start with `row-gap` as above, so no child shrinks or stretches in the scrolling body and a long word never widens it; the Sheet's rules that take the outer margins off its first and last child still hold.

- The header row is the Sheet's: `space.400` at the top and sides and `space.300` between the DetailHeader and the close button, the same 12 px DetailHeader keeps between its own parts, so the close button sits beside DetailHeader's action as one more control in the row.

What axe won't catch, from the contract, each with how this component meets it:

- [ ] Everything the eye is told, the ear is told, for every shape each prop accepts: the header's badge or marker is `aria-hidden` and its line is read in the title, which names the panel; the close button's icon is `aria-hidden` and the `CloseButton` part gives it the owned label; the body scrolls by keys and screen reader at every snap point, as the Sheet makes it.
- [ ] Every landmark and named region has an accessible name: the dialog is named by DetailHeader's `h2` through `aria-labelledby` (CR-3), never by the whole header row, so the subheader and the action's names stay out of it.
- [ ] Every default label can be overridden: `closeLabel`, given to the Sheet.
- [ ] Every shape the type accepts renders, in every variant: `title`, `lineName`, `subheader`, `action` and `children` show a string, an element and a list alike, in `line` and `stop`; the `Line` and `Stop` stories' `play` tests open the panel with each.
- [ ] A hidden copy kept for animation is `aria-hidden`, and a collapsed region is `inert`: none here; a closed panel is unmounted, or `hidden` when `slotProps.portal.keepMounted` keeps it, as the Sheet does.
- [ ] Native semantics first: the `h2` from DetailHeader, `<button>` for the close button and the trigger, and Base UI's `dialog` role on the root; the panel adds only `aria-labelledby`.
- [ ] Focus moves in reading order and returns on close: the popup, then the header's action, then the close button, then the body, as they read; Base UI returns focus to the trigger or to the element focused before.
- [ ] Every target is at least `size.target-min` square: the close button, `size.control`; the action's controls keep their own sizes.
- [ ] Loops stop when the system asks for less motion: none here; the slide reads `duration.sheet`, which the tokens shorten to 0.01 ms.

## Component rules

Rules for this pattern alone, numbered so the build and the review can cite them.

- **CR-1.** The pattern imports only `Sheet`, `DetailHeader`, their props types, `PerronProvider` if it needs it, and React, as the contract's Patterns section says; never `@base-ui/react`, its `mergeProps` included, so every class is joined by hand: the part's own first, then the caller's, whether the caller's `className` is a string or Base UI's function of the part's state.
- **CR-2.** The DetailHeader is the Sheet's `title`, and the Sheet's `slotProps.title` is `{ render: <div /> }`, so the header row is the DetailHeader, then the close button, in the DOM and on screen. The DetailHeader takes `variant`, `line`, `lineName`, `title`, `subheader` and `action`, over `slotProps.header`.
- **CR-3.** The panel names its dialog from the DetailHeader's title: it passes the Sheet `aria-labelledby` set to the title's `id`, which it gives the DetailHeader through `slotProps.title.id`: the caller's `slotProps.header.slotProps.title.id` when given, else one from React's `useId`. Base UI's Drawer merges the popup's element props after its own, so this wins over the title `div`'s `id`.
- **CR-4.** The panel is never modal: it passes the Sheet no `modal`, so the Sheet keeps its default, false, with `disablePointerDismissal` and no backdrop, and the map stays usable beside it.
- **CR-5.** The root always carries `data-variant`, the default `line` included, and the Sheet's `data-anchor`. The root's class, `pn-DetailPanel`, sets its width with `anchor` `right`; the viewport's, `pn-DetailPanel-viewport`, sets `z-index.detail`; the close button's, `pn-DetailPanel-close`, centres it; and the content's, `pn-DetailPanel-content`, lays out the body, by the rows of [Tokens](#tokens). The width and the `z-index` each have the Sheet's own rule for the same property to beat, `.pn-Sheet[data-anchor='right']` and `.pn-Sheet-viewport`, so the pattern's selector carries one more class or attribute of the element than the Sheet's (`.pn-Sheet.pn-DetailPanel[data-anchor='right']`, `.pn-DetailPanel-viewport[data-anchor]`), and a `play` test proves the computed value (see [Stories](#stories)). The close button's `align-self` and the content's grid have no Sheet rule for their property: the Sheet sets the header row's `align-items`, which `align-self` overrides, and leaves the content's `display` as it is.
- **CR-6.** The pattern holds no media query for the phone, as the Sheet holds none: the screen picks `anchor` `bottom` under `breakpoint.phone`, and the snap point props reach the Sheet as given.
- **CR-7.** `onOpen`, `onClose` and `onSnapPointChange` are the Sheet's, given on unchanged, so the panel adapts no Base UI callback of its own.
- **CR-8.** The Sheet's portal renders nothing in static markup, so the contract's `OwnedStrings` story sees only the trigger. A `play` test runs `expectOwnedStrings` over the open panel's markup in `da`, with the story's `lineName`, `title`, `subheader` and body as the caller's words.
- **CR-9.** The `Line` story's args hold `defaultOpen: true`, so the contract story finds the root, the popup, with the `ref` and the `data-testid` on it.

## Do and don't

For a caller using the pattern, with the reason.

| Do                                                                                                                                       | Don't                                                                                                                                                             |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Open one detail panel at a time, controlled by `open`, for the vehicle or stop the rider picked, and clear the selection in `onClose`    | Stack a second detail panel over the first; pick the new vehicle into the open panel, so Esc still closes the topmost thing first                                 |
| Give the destination as `title` and the line's short name as `lineName`: "Husum Torv" with "5C"                                          | Put the line in the title as well, "5C Husum Torv": the panel already reads the line first, so its name would say it twice                                        |
| Put at most one control of your own in `action`, such as "Show on map", and let the panel draw its close button                          | Put a close button of your own in `action`: the panel has one, which Esc and focus return go with, and the row has room for two controls at most beside the title |
| Pick `anchor` `bottom` under `breakpoint.phone`, in the screen's code                                                                    | Expect the panel to turn into a bottom sheet on its own; it holds no media query                                                                                  |
| Compose the body from Perron's patterns and components, StopTimeline, FactList, Section and DisruptionCard, with their own `h3` headings | Put a heading of level 2 in the body: the panel's title is its `h2`, and a section inside it is an `h3`                                                           |
| Keep one primary action per panel                                                                                                        | Put a `brand` button in `action` and another in the body; a screen has one brand action at most                                                                   |
